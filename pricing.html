The founding member strategy

This is how smart creators launch. Offer your first 20-50 members a rate that's locked in forever. It builds urgency, rewards early believers, and gives you social proof fast.

1.
Pick your eventual full price. Whatever you'd charge once you have 100+ members and proven results.
2.
Discount 20-30% for founders. If your target is $97/mo, launch at $67-$79/mo for founding members.
3.
Cap it at 20-50 spots. Scarcity drives action. "First 50 members lock in this rate forever."
4.
Set a deadline. Either hit the cap or a date, whichever comes first. "Founding rate ends Friday."
5.
Skool handles grandfathering automatically. When you raise your price, existing members keep their original rate. New members see the new price. No manual work.

Real example: Nicholas Wilton (art community) launched at $30/month founding rate with 200 spots. 180 people joined (90% conversion). He later raised the price for new members.

When to raise your prices

Don't set a price and forget it. Here's when and how to increase:

✓
You hit your founding member cap. Time to raise $5-$10 for new members.
✓
Demand exceeds your capacity. If you're struggling to keep up, your price is too low.
✓
You add significant new value. New courses, coaching calls, or tools? Raise for new members.
✓
Monthly audit rule. Ask yourself each month: "If demand > supply, raise $5-$10." Small increments are painless.

Existing members always keep their original rate (Skool's grandfathering). You're only raising prices for new signups.

5 pricing mistakes to avoid

These trip up most new community builders:

1
Pitching paid membership with no activation. Direct "join my paid community" converts at 0.3-0.8%. A $67-$97 challenge converts at 8-15%. That's 10-20x better. Always run a challenge or activation experience first.
2
Undercharging because you're nervous. If coaches charge $100-$500/hour for 1:1 work, a $49-$97/month community with group coaching is absurd value. Price based on value delivered, not your comfort level.
3
Staying on the Hobby plan too long. The $9/month Hobby plan charges 10% transaction fees. At $1,300/month in revenue, you're losing more to fees than the $99/month Pro plan costs. Switch when you pass ~$1,300/mo.
4
No annual option. Annual plans retain 92% of members vs. 68% for monthly-only. Offer annual with 2 months free (standard discount). It locks in revenue and kills churn.
5
Relying on membership fees alone. The best Skool creators layer revenue: memberships + challenges ($47-$197) + courses ($199-$499 one-time) + coaching ($1,000+). Multiple streams can 2-3x your income.
