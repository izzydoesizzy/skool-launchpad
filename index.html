Name & Set Up Your Community

Don't overthink this part. You can change everything later. The goal right now is just to get it created.

  • Pick a clear name. "The Fitness Lab" beats "John's Group." Clear beats clever every time.
  • Write a one-line description. "Go from couch to 5K in 8 weeks" beats "A fitness community."
  • Upload a cover image. 1280x720px. Canva has free templates, or use the AI prompt below.
  • Add community keywords. Up to 11 keywords for Skool's discovery search.

Configure Your Settings

Before you invite anyone, take 10 minutes to get the basics right. These settings shape the experience your members will have from the moment they click "Join."

  • Set your privacy. "Public" means anyone on Skool can find you. "Private" means invite-only. Most communities start public.
  • Add membership questions. 2-3 screening questions when people request to join. Filters spam and gives you insight.
  • Set your pricing. Not sure? See the pricing section in Phase 4 below, or start free and add paid later.
  • Create 3-5 categories. Introductions, Wins, Questions, Resources, General.

Filter out spammers and people who aren't a fit 3. Feel friendly and welcoming, not like an interrogation Keep each question under 150 characters (Skool's limit). Build Your Classroom

This is where your courses live, and it's the part people overthink the most. You do NOT need 50 lessons. You need three modules. That's it.

  • Create a "Start Here" module. 3 short lessons: welcome, how this works, first action item. Under 5 minutes each.
  • Create a "Quick Wins" module. 2-3 lessons that give a visible result in 48 hours. Most important thing for retention.
  • Create your core module. 5-8 lessons covering your main methodology. One video (5-12 min) + description + downloads per lesson.

I'm building a Skool community about [YOUR TOPIC]. My members are [DESCRIBE AUDIENCE] and the main outcome is [TRANSFORMATION]. Create a Skool Classroom structure with: 1. A "Start Here" module (3 lessons: welcome, how this works, first action item) 2. A "Quick Wins" module (2-3 lessons that give a visible result in 48 hours) 3. Core curriculum modules (organize my main methodology into 3-4 modules, 5-8 lessons each) For each lesson give me: title, description (2-3 sentences), estimated video length (5-12 min), one action item, and what downloads to attach. Keep lessons focused on ONE concept each.

Set Up Gamification

One of Skool's secret weapons. Members earn points when others like their posts and comments. Points unlock levels. Levels unlock content. It drives engagement on autopilot.

  • Customize your level names. 9 levels. Don't leave them as "Level 1, Level 2." Rename them to fit your theme.
  • Lock content behind levels. Best advanced content at Level 3-4 (20-65 likes to reach). Rewards engagement.
  • Leave the leaderboard on. Friendly competition keeps people posting. 1.71% engagement rate vs. 0.84% without it.

I run a Skool community about [YOUR TOPIC] called [COMMUNITY NAME]. My vibe is [DESCRIBE THE FEEL: professional, fun, intense, casual, etc.]. Generate 9 custom level names for Skool's gamification system. They should: progress from beginner to expert/legend, fit my theme, be short (1-2 words each), feel aspirational. Give me 3 different themed sets to choose from. Points required: L1=0, L2=5, L3=20, L4=65, L5=155, L6=515, L7=2015, L8=8015, L9=33015.

Create Your Welcome Content

Nobody wants to be the first person to post in an empty room. Seed the community with content before inviting anyone. This is where the AI prompts really shine -- you can generate all of this in minutes.

  • Write and pin a welcome post. Introduce yourself, what this community is about, and the first 3 action items.
  • Seed 10-15 posts. Mix of educational content, questions, polls, and resource shares.
  • Write community guidelines. 5-7 rules. Friendly and positive, not corporate. Pin it.
  • Schedule your first live call. Even one monthly Q&A call dramatically reduces churn.

Write a pinned welcome post for my Skool community called [COMMUNITY NAME] about [TOPIC]. I'm [YOUR NAME] and I help [YOUR AUDIENCE] to [TRANSFORMATION]. Include: 1. A warm, personal welcome, 2. What this community is about, 3. What to expect, 4. Their first 3 action items (complete Start Here module, introduce yourself, one more relevant action), 5. A closing that makes them feel like they belong. Tone: warm, genuine, like welcoming someone into your home. Keep it under 300 words.

Get Your First Members

Your community is set up. Now get real people in the door. There are a million ways, but here are the ones that actually work, starting with the most organic.

The organic way (start here)

Be genuinely helpful in other Skool communities. Have a good profile. Have a clear link to your community. Then engage. A ton. People notice.

  • Engage way more than you post. Comment on other people's posts. Only share your own stuff when it's truly valuable. Don't be spammy.
  • Send personal invitations. Message 20-30 people from your network. Personal invites convert 5-10x higher than mass emails.
  • DM every new member. Ask about their goals. Point them to Start Here. This is the #1 driver of early retention.

Content and social media

  • Daily content on your chosen platform. LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram, YouTube. Consistency matters more than the platform.
  • Offer a founding member rate. First 20-50 members get a locked-in price forever. Creates urgency.

Paid ads (when you're ready)

Want to learn ads? Evelyn Weiss teaches $5/day ads. Anti-guru, super reasonable. Join her free 7-day trial traffic challenge.

Founding member strategy

Offer your first 20-50 members a rate locked in forever. Discount 20-30% off eventual full price. Set a deadline. Skool handles grandfathering automatically (existing members keep their rate when you raise prices).

Avoid these mistakes

  • ✗ Pitching paid membership with no activation experience (use a challenge instead)
  • ✗ Undercharging because you're nervous (price on value, not comfort)
  • ✗ Staying on Hobby plan past $1,300/mo revenue (Pro saves money after that)
  • ✗ No annual option (annual retains 92% vs. 68% monthly)
  • ✗ Relying on membership fees alone (layer challenges + courses + coaching)
