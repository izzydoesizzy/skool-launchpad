Name & Set Up Your Community

Alright, time to create your community. Don't overthink this part. You can change everything later. The goal right now is just to get it set up so you have something to build on.

  • Pick a clear name. Your name should say what it's about. "The Fitness Lab" beats "John's Group." Clear beats clever every time.
  • Write a one-line description. What transformation do members get? "Go from couch to 5K in 8 weeks" is better than "A fitness community."

Configure Your Settings

Before you invite anyone, take 10 minutes to get the basics right. These settings shape the experience your members will have from the moment they click "Join."

  • Set your privacy. "Public" means anyone on Skool can find you (good for growth). "Private" means invite-only (good for exclusivity). Most communities start public.
  • Add membership questions. 2-3 screening questions when people request to join. This filters spam and gives you insight into new members. Example: "What's your biggest challenge with [topic]?"
  • Set your pricing. Not sure what to charge? Check our Pricing Guide for data-backed recommendations. Short answer: start free if you have no audience, $29-$97/month if you do.
  • Create 3-5 categories. Organize your feed with categories like: Introductions, Wins, Questions, Resources, General. Members can filter posts by category.

5

Build Your Classroom

This is where your courses live, and it's probably the part people overthink the most. You do NOT need 50 lessons on day one. You need three modules. That's it. You can always add more later.

  • Create a "Start Here" module. 3 short lessons: (1) Welcome and what to expect, (2) How this community works, (3) Your first action item. Keep each under 5 minutes.
  • Create a "Quick Wins" module. 2-3 lessons that give members a visible result in their first 48 hours. This is the single most important thing for retention.
  • Create your core module. 5-8 lessons covering your main methodology. Each lesson: one video (5-12 min) + description + any downloads. Teach the essential path, not everything you know.

6

Set Up Gamification

This is one of Skool's secret weapons and most people don't set it up properly. Members earn points when others like their posts and comments. Points unlock levels. Levels unlock content. It's a flywheel that drives engagement on autopilot.

  • Customize your level names. Skool has 9 levels. Don't leave them as "Level 1, Level 2." Rename them to fit your community theme. Makes it fun and aspirational.
  • Lock content behind levels. Put your best advanced content at Level 3-4. Members need 20-65 likes to reach that, which rewards early engagement without making it feel impossible.
  • Leave the leaderboard on. It's on by default. Friendly competition keeps people posting. Communities with gamification see 1.71% engagement rates, double those without it.

How it works: 1 like = 1 point. L1=0 pts, L2=5, L3=20, L4=65, L5=155, L6=515, L7=2015, L8=8015, L9=33015. The early levels are quick to reach, which keeps new members motivated.

Create Your Welcome Content

Nobody wants to be the first person to post in an empty room. Before you invite anyone, seed the community with content so it feels alive from day one. This is where the AI prompts really shine, because you can generate all of this in minutes.

  • Write and pin a welcome post. Introduce yourself, explain what this community is about, and give new members their first 3 action items.
  • Seed 10-15 posts. Mix of educational content, questions, resource shares, and polls. This gives new members something to respond to immediately.
  • Write a community guidelines post. Keep it short. 5-7 rules. Pin it. Friendly and positive, not corporate.
  • Schedule your first live call. Use Skool's built-in Calendar. Even one monthly Q&A call dramatically reduces churn vs. communities with no live events.

Get Your First Members

Your community is set up. Now it's time to get real people in the door. There are a million ways to do this, but let me save you some time: here are the ones that actually work, starting with the most organic.

The organic way (start here)

The most organic way is the one most people skip: be genuinely helpful in other Skool communities. Have a good profile that conveys what you do. Have a clear link to your community. Then go help, encourage, and engage. A ton. People notice.

  • Engage way more than you post. Comment on other people's posts. Sprinkle in the occasional post in Skoolers or other communities, but only if you have something truly valuable to share. Don't be spammy.
  • The best members come from Skool. People already active on the platform understand how it works. Help them, and they'll become your members.
  • Send personal invitations. Message 20-30 people from your existing network. Personal invites convert 5-10x higher than mass emails.
  • DM every new member. When someone joins, send them a personal message. Ask about their goals. Point them to Start Here. This is the #1 driver of early retention.

Content and social media

Pick one platform and show up consistently. LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, whatever you prefer. A lot of successful Skool creators use YouTube.

  • Daily content on your chosen platform. Consistency matters more than which platform. Share what you're building, behind-the-scenes, and the value members get.
  • Offer a founding member rate. If your community is paid, first 20-50 members get a locked-in price forever. "50 spots at this price" or "founding rate ends Friday."

Your First Week Playbook

You're live. Members are joining. Now the real work begins: showing up consistently. Consistency matters so much more than intensity. Here's a simple weekly rhythm to follow. You can always adjust it, but this gives you a structure to lean on.

Day What to Post
MondayGoal-setting post: "What's your #1 goal this week?"
TuesdayEducational post or new classroom lesson
WednesdayPoll or discussion question about a common challenge
ThursdayMember spotlight or win celebration
FridayWeekly recap + weekend challenge
WeekendLight engagement: respond to comments, DM inactive members

The golden rule: Post 30-60 minutes before you plan to be active in the community. The best engagement happens when you respond quickly to early comments. If you post and disappear, engagement dies.
