Courses & Classroom

Structure your classroom, plan your modules, and write lesson scripts. If you're migrating content from another platform, these prompts will reorganize everything for Skool's format.

Plan your course structure

Turn your knowledge into organized Skool modules. Start here if you're building from scratch.

I'm building a Skool community about [YOUR TOPIC]. My members are [DESCRIBE AUDIENCE] and the main outcome is [TRANSFORMATION]. Create a Skool Classroom structure with: 1. A "Start Here" module (3 lessons: welcome, how this works, first action item) 2. A "Quick Wins" module (2-3 lessons that give a visible result in 48 hours) 3. Core curriculum modules (organize my main methodology into 3-4 modules, 5-8 lessons each) For each lesson give me: - Title - Description (2-3 sentences) - Estimated video length (5-12 min) - One action item for the member - What downloads or resources to attach (if any) Keep lessons focused on ONE concept each. Better to have more short lessons than fewer long ones.

Generate 15 seed posts

Posts to fill your community before anyone joins. Nobody wants to be the first person to post in an empty room.

Write 15 seed posts for my new Skool community about [YOUR TOPIC]. These will be posted before I invite anyone, so the community feels alive on day one. Mix of: - 3 educational/value posts (teach something useful) - 3 question/discussion posts (ask something members will want to answer) - 2 resource posts (share a template, tool, or checklist) - 2 personal story posts (share a lesson learned, behind-the-scenes) - 2 poll posts (3-5 options each) - 2 celebration/motivation posts - 1 "introduce yourself" prompt Each post should: - Be written in first person (as the community owner) - End with a question or call-to-action to invite responses - Feel natural and conversational, not corporate - Vary in length (some 3 sentences, some 2 paragraphs) Assign each post to a Skool category: Introductions, Wins, Questions, Resources, or General.

Onboarding & Welcome

60-80% of new members are lost in the first week because of bad onboarding. These prompts make sure yours is rock solid.

Write a pinned welcome post

The first thing every new member sees. Pin this to the top of your community feed.

Write a pinned welcome post for my Skool community called [COMMUNITY NAME] about [TOPIC]. I'm [YOUR NAME] and I help [AUDIENCE] to [TRANSFORMATION]. Include: 1. A warm, personal welcome (who I am, why I built this) 2. What this community is about (the transformation members will get) 3. What to expect (content, calls, challenges, etc.) 4. Their first 3 action items: - Complete the "Start Here" module in the Classroom - Introduce yourself in the comments (with a specific prompt to answer) - [ONE MORE RELEVANT ACTION] 5. A closing that makes them feel like they belong Tone: warm, genuine, slightly excited. Like welcoming someone into your home, not a corporate onboarding email. Keep it under 300 words.

A detailed image description I can paste into an AI image tool (Midjourney, DALL-E, or Canva AI) to generate a 1280x720px banner. It should look professional and clearly communicate the topic. 2. A Canva-friendly alternative: describe a simple layout I can recreate manually in Canva (background color, text placement, any icons or shapes). 3. 3 text overlay options (community name + tagline) that would look good on the banner. Important: The banner should work well as a small thumbnail on mobile too. Keep text large and readable. Less is more.
