The 7-Day Skool Launch Checklist

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard · April 14, 2026

You could spend 3 months "getting ready" to launch your Skool community. Perfecting the branding. Agonizing over the name. Building 47 course modules before anyone's even joined.

Or you could launch in 7 days.

Not a sloppy, half-baked launch. A real one. With a structured classroom, gamification set up, your first members invited, and your first piece of content posted.

Here's the day-by-day plan. Block 2 to 3 hours each day and you'll be live by the end of the week.

Day 1: Set up your group (2 hours)

What you're doing: Creating your Skool account and getting the foundation in place.

Checklist:

  • Create your Skool account at skool.com
  • Name your group. Keep it clear over clever. "[Topic] Community" or "[Result] Club" works better than a creative name nobody understands. You can change it later.
  • Write your group description. 2 to 3 sentences: what the community is about, who it's for, and what members get out of it. Think about what someone would search for.
  • Upload a group photo. A clean image that represents your topic. Doesn't need to be custom designed. A good stock photo or Canva graphic works fine for now.
  • Choose your pricing model. Free to start (recommended) or paid from day one. If you're not sure, start free. You can always add a paid tier later.

Common mistake: Spending 4 hours on the perfect name. Pick something clear and move on. Nobody joins a community because of the name. They join because of the value inside.

Day 2: Build your "Start Here" module (2 to 3 hours)

What you're doing: Creating the first thing new members see when they open the classroom.

Checklist:

  • Create a "Start Here" course in the classroom. This is your onboarding flow.
  • Lesson 1: Welcome. A short video (2 to 3 minutes) or written post. Introduce yourself, explain what the community is about, and tell members what to do first. Keep it warm and direct.
  • Lesson 2: How this community works. Explain the community feed, the classroom, the calendar. Show people where things are so they're not confused.
  • Lesson 3: Your first action. Give them one thing to do right now. "Introduce yourself in the community feed" or "Complete this 5-minute exercise." This gets them engaged immediately.
  • Set up community categories. Create 3 to 5 categories for the community feed. Keep it simple: "Introductions," "Wins," "Questions," and "Resources" is a solid starting set. You can add more later.

Common mistake: Building 10 course modules before anyone joins. You only need the Start Here module for launch. Build the rest based on what your members actually ask for.

Day 3: Configure gamification and calendar (1.5 to 2 hours)

What you're doing: Setting up the systems that keep people coming back.

Checklist:

  • Set up levels. Skool lets you create levels that members unlock as they earn points. Start with 5 to 7 levels. Name them something fun that fits your niche.
  • Configure point values. How many points for posting? Commenting? Completing a lesson? Default values work fine to start. You can tune later.
  • Add level rewards. Unlock access to bonus content, a private channel, or a 1-on-1 call at higher levels. This gives members a reason to participate.
  • Set up your calendar. Add at least one recurring event. A weekly Q&A call, a monthly workshop, or even a casual "office hours" session. Having an event on the calendar makes the community feel alive.

Common mistake: Overcomplicating the level system. You don't need 15 levels with different rewards. Keep it simple. 5 levels, a couple of unlocks, done.

Day 4: Create your welcome post and community guidelines (1.5 hours)

What you're doing: Preparing the content that sets the tone for your community.

Checklist:

  • Write your welcome post. This is the pinned post at the top of the community feed. It should cover: who you are, what this community is about, and what to do first (complete the Start Here module). Keep it friendly and short. 150 to 200 words max.
  • Write your community guidelines. 5 to 7 rules. Be specific. "Be respectful" is too vague. "No self-promotion without adding value first" is clear. Other good ones: "Share wins, big or small," "Ask questions freely," "No spam or affiliate links."
  • Record a short intro video (optional but powerful). A 60 to 90 second video of you talking to the camera. Welcome people. Show them you're a real person. This builds trust faster than any written post.

Common mistake: Writing a 1,000-word welcome post that nobody reads. Short and warm beats long and thorough. Get them into the Start Here module quickly.

Day 5: Invite your first 10 to 20 people (2 hours)

What you're doing: Getting real humans into your community before you go public.

Checklist:

  • Make a list of 20 to 30 people you know who would benefit from your community. Friends, colleagues, past clients, people from other communities.
  • Send personal invitations. Not a mass email. Individual messages. "Hey [name], I built a free community about [topic] and I think you'd really like it. Want to check it out?" Include the link.
  • Create an early-member offer. Give them something for being first: input on what you build, a free 1-on-1 call, locked pricing when you go paid. Make them feel like insiders, not guinea pigs.
  • Ask for feedback. Once they're in, ask: "What do you think? Anything confusing? What would make this more useful for you?" This feedback is gold.

Common mistake: Waiting until everything is "perfect" to invite anyone. Your first members don't expect perfection. They expect to be part of something early and to have your attention. That's more valuable than polished course content.

Day 6: Post your first piece of valuable content (1.5 hours)

What you're doing: Showing your early members what the community experience looks like.

Checklist:

  • Post one high-value piece of content in the community feed. A how-to guide, a framework, a lesson, a case study. Something that makes members think "this alone was worth joining."
  • Engage with every response. Reply to every comment. Ask follow-up questions. Make it a conversation, not a broadcast.
  • Create a discussion prompt. Post a question that invites members to share. "What's your biggest challenge with [topic] right now?" or "What's one win you've had this week?" This gets people talking to each other, not just to you.
  • Welcome new members individually. When someone joins, tag them in the welcome post or DM them. "Hey, welcome! Glad you're here. Make sure to check out the Start Here module."

Common mistake: Posting content and then disappearing. The first week is about energy and presence. Show up. Respond fast. Make people feel like they landed somewhere active and alive.

Day 7: Announce publicly and go live (2 to 3 hours)

What you're doing: Opening the doors to the world.

Checklist:

  • Post your announcement on every platform you use. LinkedIn, X, Instagram, TikTok, email list. Don't just say "I made a community." Share why you built it, who it's for, and what people will get out of joining. Include the link.
  • Go live. Host a kickoff call or Q&A inside your Skool community using the calendar event feature. This does two things: gives people a reason to join now (not "someday"), and shows the community is active and real.
  • Ask your early members to spread the word. "If you know someone who'd benefit from this, would you share the link with them?" People are happy to do this when they're genuinely enjoying the community.
  • Set up your Skool discovery listing. Make sure your group is public and your description is keyword-rich so people can find you through Skool's built-in search.

Common mistake: Treating launch day as a one-time event. It's not. Keep announcing, keep sharing, keep inviting. Your "launch" is really the first day of ongoing promotion.

What to do in Week 2 (and beyond)

You launched. You have members. Now what?

Week 2 priorities:

  • Post content 3 to 5 times per week. Mix it up: how-to posts, discussion prompts, quick tips, personal stories. Variety keeps the feed interesting.
  • Host your first recurring event. A weekly live call gives members something to look forward to and a reason to check back.
  • Start building your second classroom module. Now that you have real members, build based on what they're actually asking about. Not what you guessed they'd want.
  • Keep inviting. Aim for 5 to 10 new members per week through personal outreach, social media, and discovery. Consistent growth beats viral moments.
  • Engage daily. 15 to 20 minutes per day responding to posts, welcoming new members, and starting conversations. This is what keeps a community alive.

The first month is about building habits. Your content habit. Your engagement habit. Your promotion habit. Get those three dialed in and your community will grow on its own.

You don't need 10,000 members. You don't need a viral launch. You need 7 days of focused work and the willingness to show up consistently after that.
