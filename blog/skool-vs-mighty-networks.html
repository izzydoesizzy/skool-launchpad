Skool vs Mighty Networks: Full Comparison

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard · April 14, 2026

Skool and Mighty Networks are both community platforms. They both let you host members, run courses, and charge subscriptions. But they're built on completely different philosophies.

Skool is the "keep it simple" option. Everything works out of the box. No design decisions. No custom coding. You sign up, you launch.

Mighty Networks is the "build your own world" option. Branded apps, custom layouts, spaces within spaces. More control, more complexity.

So which one do you actually need? Let's break it down.

The core difference

Mighty Networks wants to be your branded platform. Your own app in the App Store. Your own colors, fonts, and layout. Think of it as a white-label community builder where members might not even realize they're "on Mighty Networks."

Skool wants to be the simplest way to run a paid community. One feed. One course area. One calendar. Gamification built in. No branding decisions to make because the interface is the same for everyone.

This is the fork in the road. Everything else flows from this choice: do you want maximum customization, or maximum simplicity?

Feature comparison

Let's go category by category.

Community

Both platforms give you a community feed where members can post, comment, and interact. Mighty Networks organizes things into "Spaces," which are sub-communities inside your main network. You can have a Space for announcements, another for introductions, another for a specific topic. It's flexible.

Skool keeps it flatter. You get one community feed per group. You can use categories to organize posts, but there's no nested sub-community structure. For most creators, this is plenty. For larger organizations running multiple programs under one roof, Mighty's Spaces model gives you more room.

Courses

Both platforms include course hosting. Skool's course builder is straightforward: modules, lessons, video uploads. It gets the job done without fuss.

Mighty Networks also offers courses, but only on the Courses plan ($99/month) and above. Their course builder supports more content types and can be woven into the community experience more tightly. If you want courses bundled into specific Spaces or learning paths, Mighty gives you that structure.

Events and live calls

Skool has a built-in calendar for scheduling events. Members see upcoming calls right inside the group. Simple and visible.

Mighty Networks also supports events and can integrate with Zoom or other tools. Both platforms handle this reasonably well. No major winner here.

Gamification

This is where Skool pulls ahead. Every Skool group has a built-in leaderboard. Members earn points for posting, commenting, and completing courses. You can unlock levels that grant access to bonus content. It turns participation into a game, and it works. Members engage more because they can see their rank.

Mighty Networks doesn't have native gamification. No leaderboard. No points. No unlockable levels. If gamification matters to you, Skool wins this category outright.

Customization and branding

Mighty Networks wins here, and it's not close. You can customize colors, logos, layouts, and navigation. On the Mighty Pro plan, you can publish your own branded iOS and Android app. Your community looks like your brand, not like "a Mighty Networks community."

Skool has minimal customization. You can set a group icon, banner, and description. That's about it. Every Skool group looks like a Skool group. For some creators, that's a dealbreaker. For others, it's a relief: one less thing to worry about.

Discovery

Skool has a built-in community discovery page. When someone searches for communities on Skool, yours can show up. It's free organic traffic from people already looking for what you teach. Not a flood of leads, but a nice bonus.

Mighty Networks doesn't have a comparable discovery feature. Your growth depends entirely on your own marketing.

Pricing breakdown

This is where things get interesting.

Skool keeps pricing dead simple:

  • Hobby: $9/month. Unlimited members, courses, and videos. One admin. 10% transaction fee on paid memberships.
  • Pro: $99/month. Same unlimited features. Unlimited admins. Transaction fee drops to 2.9%.

That's it. Two plans. No annual billing tricks. No feature gating on the essentials.

Mighty Networks has four tiers (annual pricing):

  • Community: $41/month. Basic community features. No courses.
  • Courses: $99/month. Adds course creation and selling.
  • Business: $179/month. Adds advanced branding, workflows, and analytics.
  • Growth: $360/month. More streaming, more storage, more video capacity.
  • Mighty Pro: Custom pricing. Your own branded app in the App Store. Expect to pay around $28,000/year for this tier.

If you just want community plus courses, you're comparing Skool at $9-$99/month against Mighty at $99-$179/month. Skool is significantly cheaper at every comparable feature level.

Monthly billing on Mighty is even pricier. The Community plan jumps to $49/month and the Business plan to $219/month if you don't commit annually.

The app question

This deserves its own section because it's the number one reason people consider Mighty Networks over Skool.

Mighty Networks lets you publish a branded mobile app. Your name. Your icon. Your colors. Listed in the Apple App Store and Google Play like any other app. For established brands with large audiences, this is a legitimate selling point. It feels premium. It feels "real."

But let's be honest about what this costs. The Mighty Pro plan runs around $28,000 per year. That's a serious investment. And the app itself is still built on Mighty's infrastructure, so you're not getting a fully custom native app. You're getting Mighty Networks with your paint job on it.

Skool has a mobile app, but it's generic. Every Skool community lives inside the same Skool app. Your members download "Skool," then find your group inside it. No custom branding. No App Store listing with your name.

For most solo creators and small teams, the branded app is a nice-to-have, not a need-to-have. Your members will use whatever app you point them to. They care about the content and the community, not whether the app icon matches your brand colors.

But if you're a corporate training company or an established brand with tens of thousands of members, the branded app might genuinely matter to you.

Who picks Skool

Skool is the right choice if you value:

  • Simplicity. You want to launch fast and not spend weeks customizing your platform. Sign up, set up your group, invite members. Done.
  • Lower cost. Starting at $9/month with no annual commitment, Skool is the most affordable option for new community builders.
  • Gamification. The leaderboard and points system is unique to Skool. If you want members competing, engaging, and unlocking content, no other platform does this natively.
  • Built-in discovery. Free exposure to people already browsing Skool for communities like yours.
  • Coaching and membership businesses. If your model is "pay monthly, get access to me and a community," Skool was designed for exactly that.

Who picks Mighty Networks

Mighty Networks is the right choice if you need:

  • A branded app. If having your own app in the App Store is a real business requirement (not just a vanity play), Mighty Pro is one of the few platforms that offers this.
  • Deep customization. Custom colors, layouts, navigation structures, and sub-communities. If your brand identity matters more than speed to launch, Mighty delivers.
  • Corporate or enterprise training. Organizations running structured learning programs across departments benefit from Mighty's Spaces and organizational tools.
  • Multiple distinct sub-communities. If you need separate spaces within one network (think: different cohorts, topics, or membership tiers all under one roof), Mighty's architecture handles this better.

The bottom line

Most solo creators, coaches, and small membership businesses will be better served by Skool. It's cheaper, faster to set up, and the gamification features genuinely drive engagement. You can be live and accepting members in under an hour.

Mighty Networks makes sense for larger operations that need branding control, sub-community architecture, or a custom app. But that power comes with higher costs and a steeper learning curve.

My suggestion: if you're not sure, start with Skool. It's $9/month to test. You'll know within a week whether it has what you need. If you outgrow it, you can always migrate. But most people don't outgrow it. They just build.
