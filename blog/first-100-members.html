How to Get Your First 100 Skool Members

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard · April 14, 2026

You launched your Skool community. It looks great. The classroom is set up, the welcome post is pinned, and everything is ready.

Now you're staring at a member count of 1. Just you.

Getting your first 100 members is the hardest stretch of building a community. Not because the tactics are complicated. Because it feels slow. It feels quiet. And every day you check the number and wonder if anyone cares.

They do. You just need to go find them. Here's how.

First, a mindset reset: your first 10 matter more than 100

Before we get tactical, let's talk about the part most guides skip.

Your first 10 members are more important than the next 90. These are the people who show up early, start conversations, and set the tone for everyone who comes after. If those 10 people have a great experience, they'll tell others. If they join and see a ghost town with no replies, they'll leave quietly and never come back.

So don't rush past the early stage. Pour your energy into making those first members feel seen. Reply to every post. DM them to say thanks. Ask what they need. The growth comes from that foundation.

Realistic timeline: most people hit 100 members in 2 to 4 months. Some do it faster if they already have an audience. Some take longer if they're starting from zero. Both are fine. This is a marathon, not a launch day.

1. Start with your existing network (even if it's tiny)

You know more people than you think.

Open your phone contacts, your email, your DMs. Think about friends, colleagues, former clients, people from other communities you're in. You're not looking for thousands. You're looking for 10 to 20 people who would genuinely benefit from what you're building.

Send them a personal message. Not a mass blast. Something like:

"Hey, I'm building a free community for [topic]. I think you'd get a lot out of it. Would you want to check it out? Here's the link."

That's it. No pitch. No pressure. Just a direct invitation from someone they already know.

If 15 people say yes, you now have 15 members. That's 15% of your goal from one afternoon of DMs.

2. Share on social media without being spammy

The wrong way: posting "JOIN MY COMMUNITY" every day with a link and nothing else.

The right way: sharing the kind of content your community is about, then mentioning that you're building a space to go deeper on it.

Here's what works:

  • Share a takeaway from a conversation in your community. "Someone in my group asked about X today, and the discussion was 🔥. If you want in, link in bio."
  • Post a mini-lesson or tip, then point to the community. Give value first. The community is where people go for more.
  • Screenshot a great discussion (with permission) and share it. Social proof is more powerful than any pitch.

Do this 2 to 3 times per week across whatever platforms you use. LinkedIn, X, Instagram, TikTok. Wherever your people hang out.

3. Use Skool's discovery feature

Skool has a built-in search and discovery page where people browse communities by topic. It's not going to flood you with members overnight, but it brings in a steady trickle of people who are actively looking for what you offer.

To rank well in discovery:

  • Write a clear, keyword-rich group description. Think about what someone would search for.
  • Keep your community active. Skool ranks active groups higher.
  • Get members to engage. Comments, likes, and posts all signal to Skool that your group is worth showing.

Discovery alone probably won't get you to 100. But it compounds over time. A few members per week adds up.

4. Cross-promote with other Skool communities

This one is underrated.

Find 3 to 5 Skool communities in related (not competing) niches. Join them. Be genuinely helpful. Build relationships with the admins.

Then propose a cross-promotion: you mention their community to your members, they mention yours. Or do a joint live event. Or a guest lesson in each other's classrooms.

This works because the people in those communities are already Skool users. They understand the platform. They're comfortable joining another group. The friction is almost zero.

5. Run a free challenge or event

Nothing fills a community faster than a time-bound event with a clear outcome.

"Join our 5-day LinkedIn makeover challenge" is more compelling than "join our community about LinkedIn." The challenge gives people a reason to show up now instead of bookmarking it for later (which means never).

Keep it simple:

  • 5 to 7 days long
  • One small daily action (15 to 20 minutes)
  • A specific result at the end ("you'll have a new LinkedIn headline and about section")
  • Run it inside your Skool community so participants join to access it

Promote the challenge on social media and to your email list (even if it's 50 people). A well-run challenge can bring in 30 to 50 members in a single week.

6. Create an early-member offer

If you're planning to eventually charge for your community, give your early members something extra for joining now.

This could be:

  • A locked price. "Join now at $29/month. Price goes to $49 when we hit 100 members."
  • Bonus access. A private group call, an extra course module, direct message access to you.
  • Input on what gets built. "You'll help shape what this community becomes."

People like being early to things. They like feeling like insiders. Give them a real reason to join now instead of waiting.

7. Guest appearances: podcasts, YouTube, and lives

You don't need your own audience. You can borrow someone else's.

Pitch yourself as a guest on podcasts and YouTube channels in your niche. Focus on shows with small to medium audiences (500 to 5,000 listeners). They're more likely to say yes, and their audiences are often more engaged than big shows.

At the end of every appearance, mention your free community. Something natural like: "If anyone wants to keep the conversation going, I run a free group on Skool where we dig into this stuff every day. I'll drop the link."

One good podcast appearance can bring in 10 to 30 members. Do 5 of those over 2 months and you're well on your way.

8. LinkedIn and social content that drives to your community

LinkedIn deserves its own section because it's one of the most effective channels for Skool growth right now, especially if your community is business or career focused.

The playbook:

  1. Post 3 to 5 times per week on topics related to your community. Teach something. Share a story. Give an opinion.
  2. Put "Free community" or your Skool link in your LinkedIn bio. People check bios after reading a good post.
  3. End posts with a soft call to action. "I break this down in more detail inside my free community. Link in the comments." Then drop the link in the first comment.
  4. Engage with other people's posts. Leave thoughtful comments on posts in your niche. People click through to your profile, see your community link, and join.

This isn't fast. It's consistent. Two months of steady LinkedIn activity can drive 20 to 40 members on its own, and it keeps working long after you post.

Putting it all together

You don't need all 8 of these. Pick 3 or 4 that match your strengths and go deep on them.

Here's what a realistic path to 100 members might look like:

  • Week 1: Personal invitations to your network. 15 to 20 members.
  • Weeks 2 to 4: Start posting on social media consistently. Discovery starts to kick in. 20 to 30 more members.
  • Month 2: Run a free challenge. Guest on 2 to 3 podcasts. Cross-promote with another community. 30 to 50 more members.

That's 100 members in about 8 weeks. Not overnight. But real, engaged people who chose to be there.

The secret nobody talks about: the first 100 are the hardest. After that, your existing members start doing the marketing for you. They tell friends. They share posts. They bring people in because they genuinely like being there.

Get to 100 and the next 100 come faster. Get started today. 👊
