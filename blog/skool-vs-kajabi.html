Skool vs Kajabi: Which Platform Is Right for You?

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard·April 14, 2026

Skool and Kajabi show up in the same conversations, but they're solving different problems. Comparing them straight across is like comparing a sports car to an SUV. Both get you from A to B, but they're built for different roads.

Skool is a community platform that happens to include courses. Kajabi is a marketing platform that happens to include community. That difference shapes everything: the features, the pricing, the learning curve, and who gets the most out of each one.

Let's break it down honestly.

What each platform does best

Skool is community-first. The whole platform revolves around getting people to talk, engage, and stick around. You get a community feed, a classroom for courses, a calendar for events, and a leaderboard with gamification baked in. Four tabs. That's the entire product.

It's intentionally simple. You can set up a Skool community in under an hour and start accepting members the same day.

Kajabi is marketing-first. It's an all-in-one business platform that bundles course hosting, a website builder, email marketing, sales funnels, landing pages, automation workflows, and payment processing under one roof. Community was added later as an extra feature, not the core product.

If Skool is a community center with a classroom in the back, Kajabi is a full marketing department with a community lounge attached.

The pattern is clear. Kajabi gives you more tools. Skool gives you fewer tools that work together tightly around community engagement.

The pricing gap is real

This is where the conversation gets interesting.

Skool pricing (2026):

  • Hobby: $9/mo. Unlimited members, courses, and community. 10% + $0.30 transaction fee. 1 admin
  • Pro: $99/mo. Same features plus lower transaction fees (2.9% + $0.30), unlimited admins, custom URL, ability to hide suggested communities

Kajabi pricing (2026):

  • Basic: $179/mo ($143/mo annual). 3 products, 1 community, 10,000 contacts
  • Growth: $249/mo ($199/mo annual). 50 products, 1 community, 25,000 contacts
  • Pro: $499/mo ($399/mo annual). Unlimited products, 3 communities, 100,000 contacts

Even Kajabi's cheapest plan costs nearly double Skool's Pro plan. And Kajabi raised prices in January 2026, making the gap wider than it used to be.

But here's the thing: Kajabi includes email marketing, landing pages, and funnels. If you're using Skool, you'll likely pay for those separately. ConvertKit or Mailchimp runs $20-60/mo. A landing page tool like Carrd is $19/yr. So the real comparison is Skool + your email tool vs. Kajabi all-in-one.

Even with those extras, Skool usually comes out significantly cheaper. But the gap narrows the more tools you stack on top.

The gamification advantage

This is Skool's secret weapon and the thing Kajabi can't match.

Skool's gamification system gives members points for posting, commenting, and completing lessons. Points add up. Members level up. You choose what each level unlocks: bonus content, private channels, access to calls, whatever you want.

This creates a loop. People participate because they see progress. Progress motivates more participation. The result: higher engagement, longer retention, less churn.

Kajabi has member badges. That's about it. No leaderboard. No point system. No unlock mechanics. For a community platform, engagement infrastructure matters more than almost anything else. And Skool has it built into the DNA of the product.

The Skool discovery marketplace

Skool has something Kajabi doesn't offer at all: a built-in marketplace where people browse and discover communities.

When someone searches for fitness coaching or business strategy on Skool, your community can show up. That's organic traffic you didn't pay for. You didn't write a blog post, run an ad, or build a funnel. People found you because the platform surfaced your group.

On Kajabi, you drive 100% of your own traffic. Every visitor comes from your marketing efforts: your emails, your ads, your social media, your content. That's fine if you already have an audience and a marketing engine. But if you're starting fresh, the Skool marketplace gives you a head start that Kajabi simply can't.

Where Kajabi wins

Let's be fair. Kajabi does several things that Skool doesn't even attempt.

Email marketing. Kajabi's built-in email system handles broadcasts, automated sequences, tagging, and segmentation. On Skool, you need a separate email tool. If email is the backbone of your business, that matters.

Funnels and landing pages. Kajabi lets you build full sales funnels with checkout pages, upsells, downsells, and order bumps. You can design landing pages from templates without touching code. Skool has no page builder at all.

Advanced course features. Kajabi supports drip content (releasing lessons on a schedule), quizzes, assessments, certificates, and cohort-based courses. Skool's classroom is simpler: modules, lessons, done. If you need sophisticated course structures, Kajabi has the edge.

Standalone digital products. Kajabi lets you sell ebooks, downloads, podcasts, newsletters, and coaching sessions as individual products. Skool is built around community membership. Everything lives inside the community.

Who should choose Skool

Skool is the right pick if:

  • Community engagement is the core of your business, not just a bonus
  • You want to launch fast without spending weeks on setup
  • You're budget-conscious and want to start at $9/mo
  • You want built-in gamification to keep members active
  • You'd benefit from the Skool marketplace for organic discovery
  • You're a coach, creator, or educator running a membership or group program
  • You're comfortable using a separate email tool

Who should choose Kajabi

Kajabi makes more sense if:

  • You need email marketing, funnels, and a website all in one place
  • You sell multiple standalone digital products (not just memberships)
  • You want advanced course features like drip content, cohorts, and certificates
  • You already have an audience and need a sophisticated marketing engine
  • You're willing to pay a premium for the convenience of one platform doing everything
  • Community is a nice-to-have, not the main event

The bottom line

If you're building a business around community, Skool is purpose-built for that. It's simpler, cheaper, and the engagement tools are better. You'll need to handle email and landing pages separately, but you'll spend less overall and get a tighter community experience.

If you're building a marketing-driven course business and community is just one piece of a bigger puzzle, Kajabi gives you the full toolkit in one place. You'll pay more, and the community features aren't as strong. But you won't need to juggle multiple platforms.

Neither platform is objectively better. They're different tools for different jobs. Pick the one that matches how you actually want to run your business.
