Migration Guide

Switching from Facebook Groups to Skool

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard · April 14, 2026

You built your community on Facebook. Maybe you've got 500 members. Maybe 5,000. Maybe 50,000. And for a while, it worked. Free, easy to start, everyone already had an account.

But something shifted. Posts stopped getting seen. Engagement dropped. You started wondering: am I building on rented land?

If you're thinking about moving your Facebook Group to Skool, this guide covers everything. What transfers, what doesn't, how to actually pull it off, and what kind of migration rate to expect. No sugarcoating.

Why people leave Facebook Groups

Nobody wakes up and decides to migrate a community for fun. There's always a breaking point. Here are the ones I hear most often:

The algorithm buries your posts. You write something great. You hit publish. And maybe 15% of your members see it. Facebook decides what gets shown, not you. Your own group. Your own content. And the platform's algorithm is the gatekeeper. That's a wild setup when you think about it.

No monetization tools. Facebook Groups have no built-in way to charge for access. No payment processing. No subscription management. If you want to run a paid community, you need a third-party tool to handle billing, then manually add and remove members. It's duct tape.

No course hosting. Want to deliver a structured curriculum alongside your community? On Facebook, you're posting PDFs in the Files tab or linking to a separate platform. There's no classroom, no modules, no progress tracking. You end up paying for Teachable or Kajabi on top of everything else.

You don't own the member list. This is the big one. Facebook does not give you your members' email addresses. You can see names. You can see profiles. But if your group gets shut down tomorrow, you have no way to reach those people. They're gone.

Facebook can shut your group down. It happens. Policy violations you didn't know about. Mass reports from competitors. A glitch in the moderation system. Groups with tens of thousands of members have disappeared overnight. No warning, no appeal process that actually works, no backup plan.

Ads everywhere. Your members see ads in their feed, between posts, in the sidebar. You have zero control over this. Some of those ads might even be from your competitors. You're building an audience and Facebook is monetizing it.

Engagement keeps dropping. This is the slow bleed. Members join, go silent, and the group starts feeling like a ghost town. Facebook's notification system is unreliable. Members don't even know new posts exist unless they manually check the group.

What maps over from Facebook to Skool

The good news: the core activities in your Facebook Group have direct equivalents on Skool.

  • Posts become your Skool community feed. Same idea: text posts, images, videos, polls. Members can like, comment, and engage. The difference is every member sees every post. No algorithm filtering.
  • Files tab becomes the Classroom. Instead of dumping PDFs into a folder, you can organize content into structured courses with modules and lessons. Members can track their progress. Way more polished.
  • Events map to Skool's Calendar feature. Schedule live calls, workshops, Q&As. Members get notified and can RSVP.
  • Polls work the same way. Skool has built-in polls right in the community feed.
  • Member discussions carry over naturally. The conversation format is similar. People post, people reply.

The transition for your members is surprisingly smooth. If they can use Facebook, they can use Skool. The learning curve is almost flat.

What doesn't transfer

Let's be honest about what you lose.

Facebook's built-in audience of 3 billion users. On Facebook, people can discover your group through search, friend recommendations, and related group suggestions. That's a massive discovery engine. Skool has its own discovery feature (Skool Discovery) where communities get ranked, but it's a much smaller pond. You'll need to drive your own traffic through content marketing, ads, or word of mouth.

The free price tag. Facebook Groups are free to run. Always have been. Skool costs $99/month. That's a real cost, especially if you're just starting out. But if you're monetizing your community (or plan to), that $99 pays for itself with a single paid member at $99+/month.

Your post history. There's no automated way to export all your Facebook Group posts and import them into Skool. Your best content will need to be manually recreated or repurposed. Save your top-performing posts before you start the migration.

Casual lurkers. Some Facebook Group members joined years ago and forgot about it. They're not coming with you. And that's fine. You don't want dead weight. You want engaged members.

The ownership argument

This deserves its own section because it's the single biggest reason to switch.

On Facebook, you are a tenant. Meta owns the building, makes the rules, and can evict you anytime. You don't get your members' contact info. You can't email them. You can't text them. If the platform changes direction or your group gets flagged, you start from zero.

On Skool, you have every member's email address. You can export your entire member list at any time. If you ever decide to leave Skool, you take your people with you. That's ownership. That's the difference between building on land you own versus land you rent.

For anyone running a business (not just a casual hobby group), this matters more than anything else on the feature list.

Your 30-day migration strategy

Don't just flip a switch and abandon your Facebook Group. That's how you lose most of your members. Do it gradually over 30 days.

Week 1: Announce and build anticipation.

  • Post in your Facebook Group that you're moving to a new home. Explain why. Be transparent about the reasons (better experience, courses, no algorithm, you want to own the community).
  • Set up your Skool community. Add your best content to the Classroom. Make it look ready and valuable before anyone walks in the door.
  • Pin the announcement post so every member sees it.

Week 2: Start posting exclusively on Skool.

  • Share your best new content only on Skool. On Facebook, post teasers with a link to the full version on Skool.
  • Offer an incentive for early movers. A free trial of your paid tier, bonus content, a live workshop, anything that rewards people for making the switch now.
  • Post daily reminders in the Facebook Group. Not annoying ones. Valuable ones. Share screenshots of discussions happening on Skool. Create FOMO.

Week 3: Ramp up the urgency.

  • Stop responding to questions in the Facebook Group. Redirect people: "Great question! I answered this in detail over on Skool. Here's the link."
  • Share wins from members who already moved. Let them tell the story.
  • If you have a mailing list (even a small one), email your members directly with the Skool link.

Week 4: Wind down.

  • Post a final reminder in the Facebook Group.
  • Update the group description to point to your Skool community.
  • Don't delete the Facebook Group. Leave it as a signpost. Anyone who finds it in the future will see where to go. Change the cover image to show your Skool community name and link.

What migration rate to expect

Here's the honest truth: you're not bringing everyone.

Expect 20-40% of your active members to migrate. Not 20-40% of your total member count. Of your active members. The people who actually post, comment, and engage.

If you have a 10,000-member Facebook Group with 500 active members, you might bring 100-200 people to Skool. That sounds small. But 200 engaged people on Skool will generate more value than 10,000 silent ones on Facebook. Every time.

The members who don't follow you were never really "your" community anyway. They were Facebook's audience that happened to be in your group.

A few things that push your migration rate higher:

  • Strong personal brand (people follow you, not the platform)
  • An email list you can contact directly
  • An incentive worth switching for (exclusive content, free trial, live event)
  • A Skool community that's clearly better than what you had on Facebook

When to stay on Facebook

Skool isn't the right move for everyone. Keep your Facebook Group if:

  • It's purely social. A neighborhood group, a parenting circle, a local hobby club. No courses, no paid content, no business model. Facebook is fine for casual communities with no monetization plans.
  • Your audience skews older and isn't tech-adventurous. Some demographics live on Facebook and won't move. If your members are 60+ and barely comfortable with Facebook's interface, asking them to create a Skool account is a big ask.
  • You have no plans to monetize. If your community is genuinely free and you have no intention of charging, building courses, or selling products, the $99/month Skool fee doesn't make sense. Facebook's free tier does the job.
  • Discovery is your primary growth channel. If most of your new members find you through Facebook's search and recommendation engine, moving to Skool means you need a new acquisition strategy. Be ready for that.

The bottom line

Moving from Facebook Groups to Skool is trading reach for ownership. You lose the built-in audience of 3 billion people. You gain control over your member list, your content, your monetization, and your future.

For hobby groups, Facebook is fine. For anyone building a business around their community, the migration is worth every bit of effort.

Set up your Skool community first. Make it great. Then bring your people over gradually. Keep the Facebook Group alive as a signpost. And don't stress about the members who don't follow. The ones who do are the ones who matter.
