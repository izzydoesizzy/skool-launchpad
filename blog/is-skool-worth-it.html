Is Skool Worth It? An Honest Review

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard · April 14, 2026

Every Skool review you find online is written by an affiliate. Including this one. So let me be upfront about that, and then give you the most honest breakdown I can.

Skool is great for a lot of people. It's also not the right fit for everyone. Here's exactly where it shines, where it falls short, and the math on whether the cost makes sense.

The honest pros ✅

1. It's genuinely simple. This isn't marketing fluff. Skool has four features: community, courses, calendar, and gamification. That's the whole product. No feature bloat, no confusing dashboards, no settings buried three menus deep. If you've ever felt overwhelmed by Kajabi or Teachable's admin panel, Skool will feel like a relief.

2. Gamification actually works. Points, levels, and leaderboards sound gimmicky on paper. In practice, they change behavior. Members who would normally lurk start posting because they want to level up. Content you gate behind higher levels becomes a reward people work toward. It creates a participation loop that most community platforms completely lack.

3. Built-in payments with zero setup. Set a price, toggle it on. Skool handles billing, access control, and payouts. No Stripe integration to configure, no webhook nightmares, no "why isn't my checkout page working" at 11pm. It just works.

4. All-in-one means less to manage. One login. One platform. One support team if something breaks. When you're running courses on Teachable, community on Circle, calls on Calendly, and payments through Stripe, that's four potential failure points. Every tool you add is another thing that can break, update, or change its pricing on you.

5. The discovery marketplace. Skool has a built-in directory where people browse and join communities. It's not going to replace your marketing strategy, but it's free organic traffic that you don't get from any other platform. Over time, as more people use Skool, this becomes more valuable.

The honest cons ❌

1. Very limited design customization. You can't change fonts, colors, or layout. Every Skool community looks basically the same. You get a cover image, an icon, and your content. That's it. If your brand identity is a big part of how you stand out, this will frustrate you.

2. No native email marketing. Skool doesn't send newsletters, drip sequences, or broadcast emails. You can notify members inside the platform, but if you want to build an email list and run campaigns, you need a separate tool like ConvertKit, Mailchimp, or Brevo. For many creators, email is still the most reliable sales channel, and Skool doesn't touch it.

3. No built-in landing page builder. There's no way to create a polished sales page inside Skool. Your group has a description page, and that's your "landing page." It works, but it's minimal. If you rely on long-form sales pages with testimonials, countdown timers, and video embeds to convert, you'll need to build that elsewhere.

4. No custom domain. Your community lives at skool.com/your-group-name. Period. You can't host it on yourbrand.com. For some people this doesn't matter at all. For others, especially in B2B or corporate training, it's a dealbreaker.

5. No native integrations. No Zapier integration baked in. No CRM connection. No analytics beyond basic member counts. If you're running a sophisticated funnel with multiple touchpoints, Skool sits in its own silo. You can work around this, but it's extra effort.

Who it's PERFECT for

  • Coaches and consultants who deliver group programs and want one place for content, community, and calls
  • Course creators who want community around their content, not just a one-and-done purchase
  • People who are tired of tool sprawl and want to simplify their stack down to one platform
  • Anyone who values speed over perfection. You can launch a Skool community in an afternoon. Try that with Kajabi.
  • Creators just getting started who don't have (or want) a complex tech setup

Who should look elsewhere

  • People who need heavy branding and customization. If your business depends on a pixel-perfect, fully branded experience, Skool's one-size-fits-all design won't cut it. Look at Circle, Mighty Networks, or a custom build.
  • E-commerce sellers. Skool is for knowledge and community, not physical or digital product shops. Shopify is a different world.
  • Advanced marketers who need email automation. If email sequences, tagging, and segmentation are core to your business model, you'll need a separate email platform regardless, and the lack of native integration adds friction.
  • Corporate or enterprise training teams. No custom domain, no SSO, no advanced user management. If you need to deploy this for a company of 500 employees, Skool isn't built for that.

That's a savings of $40/month, or $480/year, just on subscription costs. And that's using modest pricing tiers for each tool. If you're on higher plans, the gap widens.

But the real savings isn't the dollars. It's the time. Managing four separate platforms, keeping integrations working, dealing with four support teams when things break. That overhead adds up to hours every month. Hours you could spend creating content, coaching clients, or growing your audience.

If you're just starting out and your revenue is under $1,200/month, the Hobby plan at $9/month is even more compelling. You're paying less than a Netflix subscription for a fully functional community and course platform. The 10% transaction fee stings a little, but at low volume it's still cheaper than Pro.

The verdict

Skool is worth it if you want simplicity and you're okay with trading customization for speed. It's the fastest path from "I have an idea for a community" to "I have a live, paying community." Nothing else comes close on setup speed and ease of use.

It's NOT worth it if you need deep branding, email marketing, advanced analytics, or enterprise features. Those gaps are real, and no amount of gamification points makes up for missing core tools in your workflow.

For most coaches, course creators, and community builders? Skool is probably the best $99/month (or $9/month) you'll spend. The simplicity lets you focus on what actually matters: helping your people and growing your business.

The 14-day free trial means you can build your whole community, test every feature, and decide before you pay anything. That's the move. Try it, see if it clicks, and go from there.
