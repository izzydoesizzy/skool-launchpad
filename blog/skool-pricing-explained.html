Skool Pricing Explained: Hobby vs Pro Plan in 2026

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard · April 14, 2026

Skool keeps pricing simple. Two plans. No annual vs. monthly mind games. No hidden tiers buried three clicks deep.

But there's a real decision to make here, because the transaction fees on each plan change your math completely as you grow. Let's walk through exactly what you're paying for and when it makes sense to upgrade.

The two plans at a glance

Hobby: $9/month. This is your starting point. You get everything you need to launch a community: community feed, classroom (courses), calendar and events, gamification with points and leaderboards, built-in payments, unlimited members, unlimited videos, unlimited live streaming, and the mobile app. One admin seat.

Pro: $99/month. Everything in Hobby plus up to 30 admin seats, member referral/affiliate tools, advanced automation integrations (AutoDM, Zapier), and ownership transfer. The big difference, though, is the transaction fee.

Both plans come with a 14-day free trial. Credit card required to start, but you won't be charged until day 15.

The transaction fee difference (this is the important part)

Here's where people get tripped up.

Hobby charges 10% + $0.30 per transaction. That means for every $49 membership payment you collect, Skool takes $5.20. On a $97 payment, they take $10.00.

Pro charges 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction. That same $49 payment costs you $1.72. The $97 payment costs $3.11.

That's a massive difference. And it scales. Let's say you have 30 members paying $49/month. That's $1,470 in gross revenue.

  • On Hobby: You pay $9 (plan) + $156 (10% of $1,470 + $0.30 x 30) = $165/month in total costs. You keep $1,305.
  • On Pro: You pay $99 (plan) + $51.63 (2.9% of $1,470 + $0.30 x 30) = $150.63/month in total costs. You keep $1,319.

At roughly $1,300/month in gross revenue, Pro starts saving you money. Below that, Hobby is cheaper. Above that, Pro pulls further ahead with every dollar you earn.

The breakeven math

Let's make this concrete. The exact breakeven depends on your price point and number of transactions, but it lands around $1,200-$1,400/month in gross membership revenue.

Some examples:

  • $29/month membership: Pro pays for itself at about 45 members
  • $49/month membership: Pro pays for itself at about 27 members
  • $97/month membership: Pro pays for itself at about 14 members
  • $197/month membership: Pro pays for itself at about 7 members

Once you cross that line, every new member saves you more on Pro than on Hobby. At $5,000/month in revenue, you're saving over $250/month on Pro. At $10,000/month, over $600/month.

What about payment processing?

This is where Skool quietly saves you money compared to other platforms.

On most competitors, you need to connect Stripe (or another payment processor) and pay their fees on top of the platform fee. Stripe charges 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction.

Skool handles payments natively. The transaction fees listed above are your total processing cost. No separate Stripe account. No double-dipping on fees. No extra invoices to track.

That simplicity is easy to overlook, but it matters. On platforms that require external payment processing, you're often paying 2.9% to Stripe plus whatever the platform charges. On Skool, what you see is what you pay.

What both plans include

It's worth emphasizing: Skool doesn't gate core features behind the Pro plan. Both Hobby and Pro give you:

  • Community feed with posts, polls, and discussions
  • Classroom for hosting courses and modules
  • Calendar for events, calls, and workshops
  • Gamification (points, levels, leaderboards, level-locked content)
  • Built-in payment processing
  • Unlimited members
  • Unlimited video uploads
  • Unlimited live streaming
  • Mobile app access for your members
  • Skool Discovery (built-in SEO and search)

That's a lot of platform for $9/month. Most competitors charge $89+ for a comparable feature set, and some of them still limit your member count or video storage.

When to upgrade to Pro

Don't rush it. Start on Hobby and upgrade when one of these is true:

  1. Your gross revenue crosses ~$1,300/month. This is when Pro becomes cheaper than Hobby on a total-cost basis.
  2. You need team members. Hobby is one admin only. If you have a VA, a co-coach, or a community manager, you need Pro.
  3. You want member affiliates. Pro lets your members earn commissions for referring new members. Great growth tool.
  4. You're integrating with other tools. Pro unlocks AutoDM and deeper Zapier integrations for automating onboarding, follow-ups, and more.

Most people start on Hobby for 2-6 months, then upgrade when the revenue math tips. There's no penalty for switching. You just change your plan and keep going.

How Skool compares to competitors

Here's what other platforms charge as of April 2026:

  • Circle: $89/month (Professional), $199/month (Business), $419/month (Enterprise). Plus 2-4% transaction fees on top. Email add-on is another $99/month.
  • Kajabi: $143/month (Basic, annual), $199/month (Growth, annual), $399/month (Pro, annual). No transaction fees with Kajabi Payments, but the base price is 15x Skool's entry point.
  • Mighty Networks: $41/month (Community, annual), $99/month (Courses, annual), $179/month (Business, annual), $360/month (Growth). Plus Stripe processing fees on every transaction.

Skool's $9 Hobby plan is the cheapest entry point by a wide margin. And even the $99 Pro plan undercuts most competitors' mid-tier offerings while including features (like built-in payments with no external processor) that others charge extra for.

The tradeoff? Skool gives you less customization. No custom domain on your community. No white-labeling. No branded mobile app. If those things matter to you, Circle or Mighty Networks could be worth the extra cost. If you care more about getting paid members and spending less time configuring settings, Skool wins.

The bottom line

Start on Hobby at $9/month. Launch your community. Get your first paying members. When you're consistently earning over $1,300/month (or you need admin seats), upgrade to Pro.

That's it. No complicated pricing matrix. No "contact sales for a quote." Two plans, clear math, and a 14-day free trial to test everything before you pay anything. 💪
