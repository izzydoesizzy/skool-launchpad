Using Skool's Calendar & Events for Live Calls, Challenges & Workshops

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard·April 14, 2026

Content and community posts are great. But what really holds a community together is live interaction. It's the reason members stick around month after month. They don't just want information. They want connection.

Skool has a built-in calendar feature that makes running live events simple. No third-party scheduling tools. No janky integrations. Just create an event, attach a video link, and your members get notified. Let's walk through how to use it well.

What kinds of events can you run?

The calendar is flexible. Here are the event types that work best inside a Skool community:

  • Weekly live calls. The bread and butter. A standing call where you teach, answer questions, or do hot seats. This is the anchor event that keeps members coming back.
  • Q&A sessions. Open the floor. Let members bring their questions. No agenda, just answers. These are low-prep and high-value.
  • Workshops. More structured. Pick a topic, teach for 30 to 45 minutes, then open for questions. Great for diving deep into a specific skill or concept.
  • Challenge kick-offs and check-ins. Running a 5-day challenge? Put the kick-off on the calendar. Add daily check-in events. Members can see the full schedule at a glance.
  • Accountability check-ins. Weekly or bi-weekly calls where members share what they committed to and whether they followed through. Short, focused, powerful.
  • Guest expert sessions. Bring in someone with a different expertise. Your members get fresh value, and the guest gets exposure to your audience. Win-win.

You don't need all of these. Start with one weekly call and build from there.

Setting up events in the calendar

Creating an event in Skool takes about 60 seconds. Go to the Calendar tab in your community, click "Create Event," and fill in the details: title, date, time, description, and a video call link.

For the video link, you have options:

  • Zoom. The standard. Reliable, everyone knows how to use it, and the free plan handles up to 40 minutes (paid plans are unlimited).
  • Google Meet. If your members are already in the Google ecosystem, this is seamless. No downloads required.
  • Skool's native video. Skool has built-in video calling. No external tool needed. It's simple and clean, though it has fewer features than Zoom (no breakout rooms, no recording built-in). For small to mid-size calls, it works great.

Pick whatever your members are most comfortable with. Don't overthink it. The tool matters less than showing up consistently.

Recurring vs. one-time events

Skool lets you create both recurring and one-time events. Use them differently.

Recurring events are for your anchor calls. Your weekly Q&A. Your biweekly workshop. Set it once and it auto-populates on the calendar going forward. Members see a full schedule and know exactly when to show up. This consistency is what builds the habit of attendance.

One-time events are for special occasions. A guest expert call. A challenge kick-off. A bonus training on a trending topic. One-time events feel exclusive, which drives higher attendance. When something only happens once, people prioritize it.

The sweet spot for most communities: one recurring weekly call plus one or two special events per month.

Event best practices

Running events is easy. Running events that people actually attend takes a little more thought.

Keep them short and focused. Thirty to sixty minutes is the sweet spot. Anything longer and attendance drops off. If you have more to cover, split it into two sessions. A focused 30-minute call beats a meandering 90-minute one every time.

Pick a consistent day and time. "Every Tuesday at 12 PM Eastern." Not "sometime this week, I'll post when." Consistency builds habits. When your members know Tuesday at noon is their call, they block it off. When it moves around, they forget.

Record everything. Not everyone can attend live. Record your calls and post the replay inside your community or classroom. This turns every live event into evergreen content. Members who missed the call still get the value. And replays give new members a backlog of content to binge when they first join.

Start on time. End on time. Respect people's schedules. If your call is 30 minutes, end at 30 minutes. Members trust you more when you keep your promises, even the small ones.

Engage, don't lecture. The best live calls are conversations, not presentations. Ask questions. Call on people by name. Do hot seats where you work through someone's specific problem live. This is the magic that makes a live call worth showing up for instead of watching the replay.

Using events for member retention

This is the real reason to run live events. Retention.

Members cancel subscriptions when they feel like they're not getting enough value, or when they forget about you entirely. A weekly live call solves both problems. It delivers value on a predictable schedule, and it sends a notification that puts your community back on their radar every week.

Think about it from the member's perspective. If they're paying $49/month and they attend one call per week, that's roughly $12 per live session. If the calls are good, that feels like a steal. If there are no calls and all they get is access to a forum, $49 starts to feel steep.

Live events also create relationships between members. When people see the same faces every Tuesday, they start to know each other. They start chatting in the community feed between calls. They build friendships. And people don't leave communities where they have friends.

How to structure a multi-day challenge using the calendar

Challenges are one of the most effective engagement tools in any community. Here's how to set one up using Skool's calendar.

Step 1: Define the challenge. Pick a specific outcome. "Write your LinkedIn headline in 5 days." "Land your first freelance client in 7 days." "Publish 3 blog posts this week." Specific beats vague.

Step 2: Create a calendar event for each day. Day 1 kick-off call. Day 2 through 4 check-ins (these can be short, 15 to 20 minutes, or just a post prompt with no live call). Day 5 celebration and share-your-results session.

Step 3: Create a community post for the challenge. Pin it. Include the full schedule, the rules, and what members need to do each day. Link to the calendar events so members can RSVP.

Step 4: Run it. Show up for the calls. Comment on every member's progress post. Celebrate completions loudly. Tag members who are falling behind with encouragement, not pressure.

After the challenge, you'll have a burst of engagement, a group of members who feel accomplished, and a pile of success stories you can use as social proof. Run one every month or every other month and your community will never feel stale.

A note on time zones

If your community is international (and most are), time zones are going to be a thing. You can't pick a time that works for everyone. Don't try.

Here's what works instead:

  • Pick a time that works for your largest member cluster. If 70% of your members are in North America, schedule for a North American time zone. You'll never please everyone, so optimize for the majority.
  • Always post recordings. This is non-negotiable for international communities. Members in other time zones can watch the replay within 24 hours.
  • Consider alternating times. If you have significant groups in two time zones, alternate your weekly call between two times. Week 1 at 12 PM Eastern, Week 2 at 7 PM Eastern. Not perfect, but it gives more people a chance to attend live.
  • Skool displays times in each member's local time zone. So when you create an event at 12 PM Eastern, a member in London sees it as 5 PM BST. You don't need to do the math for them. The platform handles it.

Start simple

You don't need a packed calendar on day one. Start with one weekly call. Show up for it every single week. Build the habit for yourself and for your members.

Once that's dialed in, add a monthly challenge. Then maybe a guest session. Layer it on gradually. A community with one great weekly call will outperform a community with five mediocre events on the calendar.

The calendar is simple on purpose. Use it. Your members will thank you. 🎯
