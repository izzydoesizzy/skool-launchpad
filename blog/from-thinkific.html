Switching from Thinkific to Skool

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard · April 14, 2026

Thinkific is a solid course platform. Clean interface, good course builder, no transaction fees. But somewhere along the way, you start noticing what's missing. Your students finish a course and never come back. There's no real community. And the add-on costs keep piling up.

If that sounds familiar, you're not alone. A lot of course creators are making this exact switch. Here's what to expect.

Why people leave Thinkific

👥
Community is an afterthought. Thinkific added a "Communities" feature, but it's basic. No gamification, no leaderboards, no points system. It feels bolted on, not built in. Students don't have a reason to come back once they've watched the videos.
$
Pricing adds up with add-ons. Thinkific's Basic plan is $49/mo. Start is $99/mo. Grow is $199/mo. But the base plan is limited. Want to remove Thinkific branding? Upgrade. Want assignments? Upgrade. Want bundles and memberships? Upgrade again. By the time you have everything you need, you're often on the $199/mo plan.
📈
Student engagement is low. Thinkific delivers courses well. But delivering content isn't the same as building engagement. Without community, live calls, or gamification baked into the experience, students watch passively and move on.
🚧
No free plan anymore. Thinkific killed its free plan in 2026. New creators now start with a 14-day trial and then must pick a paid plan. This matters less for migration, but it shows how the platform is pushing toward higher price tiers.

What you'll lose (and workarounds)

Quiz builder. Thinkific has real quizzes with multiple question types, pass/fail logic, and grade tracking. Skool has nothing like this natively. For most community-based courses, you can use an external form tool. But if quizzes are core to your student experience, this is a real gap.

Certificates. Thinkific auto-generates branded certificates when students complete a course. On Skool, you'd handle this manually. A common workaround: use Skool's level system to recognize completion, then send a certificate via email or Canva.

Course bundles and ecommerce. Thinkific lets you bundle courses, offer upsells, and run coupons. Skool's payment model is simpler: monthly membership or one-time access. If you sell multiple standalone courses at different price points, this is a significant difference.

Custom website. Thinkific includes a basic site builder for your course school. Skool doesn't do websites. You'll need a separate landing page or website.

What you'll gain

  • Engagement that compounds. Gamification changes everything. Members earn points for posting, commenting, completing lessons. Leaderboards create friendly competition. People come back daily instead of disappearing after the last video.
  • Built-in live events. No more scheduling Zoom calls and sending links. Skool Call handles it natively, right inside your community.
  • Discovery. Skool has a marketplace where people browse and join communities. Thinkific doesn't have anything comparable for organic discovery.
  • Simplicity. One platform for community, courses, events, and payments. No add-ons to evaluate, no tiers to compare. It just works.
  • Recurring revenue by default. Skool pushes you toward the membership model, which is a more sustainable business than one-off course sales.

The real cost picture

Thinkific has zero transaction fees, which sounds great. But you're paying $49-$199/mo for courses only. Add a community tool ($49-99/mo for Circle or similar), and you're at $98-$298/mo total.

Skool Pro at $99/mo gives you courses AND community AND live events AND gamification. Yes, there's a 2.9% processing fee. On $3,000/mo in revenue, that's $87 in fees. But you're still spending less than a Thinkific Start plan plus a separate community tool.

If you're on Thinkific Grow ($199/mo), switching saves you $100/mo outright. That's $1,200/year.

How to migrate (step by step)

  1. 1
    Export your student data. In Thinkific, go to Users and export your full student list as CSV. Note which students are enrolled in which courses. You'll need this for the transition emails.
  2. 2
    Download all course content. Videos, PDFs, worksheets, supplementary materials. Thinkific lets you download your uploaded content. If you used Thinkific's video hosting, make sure you have local copies of everything.
  3. 3
    Set up your Skool community. Sign up here. Name your community, write a clear description, set up your branding. Make it feel like home before you invite anyone.
  4. 4
    Rebuild courses in Skool's Classroom. Create your modules and lessons. Upload videos (Skool hosts them natively). Attach files. The structure is simpler than Thinkific, so you may need to consolidate some content. That's usually a good thing.
  5. 5
    Set up gamification. This is the fun part. Configure your levels, decide what actions earn points, set up the leaderboard. This is the single biggest upgrade from Thinkific, so put some thought into it.
  6. 6
    Announce the migration to your students. Send a clear email. Explain what's changing and why. Highlight the upgrades: community access, live calls, gamification. Give them a direct link to join. Make it feel like an upgrade, not a disruption.
  7. 7
    Run parallel for 2 weeks. Keep Thinkific live while students transition. Post in both places. Send reminder emails on day 3, day 7, and day 12. Then shut Thinkific down with a final notice.

When to stay on Thinkific

Thinkific is the better fit if:

  • • You sell standalone courses without a community component
  • • You need native quizzes and graded assessments as part of your courses
  • • Certificate automation matters (compliance training, professional development)
  • • You sell multiple courses at different price points using bundles and upsells
  • • You want a branded course website with a custom domain

Thinkific is a course platform. Skool is a community platform with courses inside. If courses are your entire product and community is optional, Thinkific might still be the better tool.

The bottom line

Thinkific gives you a polished course experience. Skool gives you a place where people actually show up every day.

If your students watch your course and then vanish, the problem isn't your content. It's the platform. A community-first approach changes the dynamic. People stay because they're connected to other people, not just to your videos.

The migration takes a weekend of setup and two weeks of transition. On the other side, you get a platform that costs the same or less and does more of what actually moves the needle: keeping people engaged, coming back, and telling their friends.
