How to Make Money on Skool: 7 Revenue Models That Actually Work

By Izzy Piyale-Sheard · April 14, 2026

Skool isn't just a community platform. It's a revenue engine, if you know how to use it.

The creators doing well on Skool aren't relying on one income stream. They're stacking multiple models on top of each other, using the same community, the same members, and the same content.

Here are 7 revenue models that work on Skool right now, with realistic numbers and who each one is best for.

1. Monthly memberships

The foundation. The bread and butter. The thing Skool was literally built for.

You charge a monthly fee for access to your community. Members get the feed, the classroom, the events, the people. You get recurring revenue that compounds over time.

How it works on Skool: Set your price in the group settings. Skool handles payments, billing, and access. Members who stop paying automatically lose access. No Stripe account needed, no plugins, no headaches.

Realistic numbers:

  • $29-$49/month for niche communities (fitness, productivity, hobbies)
  • $49-$97/month for professional skills (marketing, sales, freelancing)
  • $97-$297/month for premium coaching communities
  • 50 members at $49/month = $2,450/month recurring
  • 100 members at $97/month = $9,700/month recurring

Best for: Anyone with ongoing value to deliver. If you can post weekly, run monthly calls, and keep a conversation going, memberships work.

The magic is in the recurring part. Fifty members doesn't sound like a lot. But $2,450/month showing up every month without launching anything new? That changes everything.

2. Paid challenges

Challenges are time-limited, action-oriented sprints. "Do this specific thing in 5 days." They create urgency. They create results. And they create revenue.

How it works on Skool: Create a separate group (or use your existing one) with a time-bound event. Use the classroom for daily lessons. Use the calendar for live kickoff and wrap-up calls. Use the community feed for accountability check-ins.

Realistic numbers:

  • $47-$197 per participant for a 5-7 day challenge
  • 30 participants at $97 = $2,910 in a week
  • Run 4 challenges per year = $11,640 in extra revenue

Best for: Creators who want cash injections without the commitment of a monthly membership. Also great as a front-end offer to bring people into a paid community afterward.

The best part? Challenges double as lead magnets. Someone who pays $97 for a challenge and gets results is 10x more likely to join your $49/month membership.

3. Course sales in the classroom

Skool's classroom feature lets you host full courses with modules, lessons, and drip content. You can sell access to these courses as a standalone offer or include them as part of your membership.

How it works on Skool: Build your course in the classroom tab. Gate it behind a payment (or a membership tier). Members work through it at their own pace or on a schedule you set. You can drip-release modules weekly if you want.

Realistic numbers:

  • $197-$997 for a self-paced course
  • $997-$2,997 for a live cohort-based course
  • 20 students at $497 = $9,940 per launch

Best for: Anyone who has a defined transformation to teach. "Go from X to Y in Z weeks." Courses work best when there's a clear outcome, not just information.

4. Group coaching add-ons

This is the "premium tier" play. Your membership gives access to the community and courses. Your coaching add-on gives access to you.

How it works on Skool: Create a separate paid group at a higher price point. Use Skool's calendar for weekly or biweekly coaching calls. Members post questions in the feed between calls. You can also do hot-seat coaching, Q&A sessions, or accountability pods.

Realistic numbers:

  • $197-$497/month for group coaching (small group, 10-20 people)
  • $497-$997/month for intensive group coaching with direct feedback
  • 12 members at $297/month = $3,564/month

Best for: Coaches, consultants, and experts who want to scale beyond 1:1 work. You can serve 12 people in one call instead of doing 12 separate sessions.

The key is capping the group. Scarcity is real here, not manufactured. You can only give meaningful attention to so many people. When the group fills up, start a waitlist. That alone sells the next cohort.

5. Affiliate revenue from Skool's referral program

This one is unique to Skool. The platform itself runs an affiliate program that pays you 40% recurring commission when someone signs up for Skool through your link.

Read that again. 40%. Recurring. For life.

How it works on Skool: Every Skool user gets a referral link. When someone creates a group through your link, you earn 40% of whatever they pay Skool every month. If they're on the Pro plan at $99/month, you earn $39.60/month. Every month they stay. Skool also attributes new groups to you automatically when members create groups from within your community.

Realistic numbers:

  • 10 referred Pro users = $396/month passive income
  • 50 referred users (mix of Hobby and Pro) = $500-$1,500/month
  • This compounds. You refer people once. You get paid monthly. Forever.

Best for: Anyone teaching business, marketing, or online entrepreneurship. If your audience is the type of person who might start their own community, this is free money sitting on the table.

Even if you teach cooking or fitness, some of your members might want to start their own group someday. A mention of "I use Skool, here's my link" in your onboarding sequence costs you nothing and can produce surprising returns.

6. Digital product sales

Templates. Swipe files. Workbooks. Spreadsheets. Notion dashboards. Prompt packs. Anything your members would pay $9-$97 for as a one-time purchase.

How it works on Skool: You can sell digital products through your community by linking to checkout pages, using the classroom for delivery, or offering them as bonuses for higher membership tiers. Some creators also use Skool's payment feature to create a "products" group where each product is a separate course module.

Realistic numbers:

  • $9-$27 for templates and simple tools
  • $27-$97 for full resource packs and bundles
  • 100 sales at $27 = $2,700 (often from one launch post)

Best for: Creators who have practical, tangible tools to share. Especially powerful when your digital product solves a specific problem your community members keep asking about.

Digital products also make great entry-level offers. Someone who buys a $17 template is much more likely to join your $49/month membership than a cold follower who's never paid you anything.

7. High-ticket coaching and consulting upsells

Your community is the trust builder. Your high-ticket offer is the profit center.

How it works on Skool: Run your community (free or paid). Build relationships. Demonstrate your expertise daily through posts, calls, and feedback. When members are ready for deeper help, offer 1:1 coaching or consulting packages. The sales conversation is warm because they already know you.

Realistic numbers:

  • $2,000-$5,000 for a 3-month 1:1 coaching package
  • $5,000-$25,000 for done-for-you consulting projects
  • 2 clients per month at $3,000 = $6,000/month from a community of 200

Best for: Consultants, agency owners, and coaches with high-value expertise. Your community does the selling for you. Every post is a demonstration of your knowledge. Every member interaction builds trust. When someone is ready for help, you're the obvious choice.

You don't need hundreds of members for this to work. A free community of 200 people in the right niche, with 2 of them converting to high-ticket clients per month, is a $72,000/year business before you count any other revenue stream.

The stacking concept: where the real money is

Here's what separates the creators earning $2K/month from the ones earning $20K/month. They don't pick one model. They stack them.

A realistic stack for a creator 6-12 months in:

  1. Free community (500 members) for discovery and trust-building
  2. Paid membership at $49/month (80 members) = $3,920/month
  3. Quarterly challenge at $97 (40 participants) = $3,880/quarter = ~$1,293/month
  4. Digital product bundle at $37 (15 sales/month) = $555/month
  5. Group coaching at $297/month (8 members) = $2,376/month
  6. Skool affiliate referrals (15 Pro users) = $594/month
  7. 2 high-ticket clients at $2,500 = $5,000/month

Total: ~$13,738/month.

Is every creator going to hit those numbers? No. But the structure is sound. Each layer feeds the next. Free members become paid members. Paid members buy courses and join challenges. Challenge graduates upgrade to coaching. Coaching clients refer new people.

You don't need to launch all seven at once. Start with one. Add a second when the first is running smoothly. Stack over time.

Where to start

If you're starting from zero, pick one:

  • Got an audience? Start with a paid membership. Simple, recurring, and predictable.
  • No audience yet? Start with a free community + a paid challenge within 60 days. Build trust fast, then monetize with urgency.
  • Already coaching 1:1? Start with group coaching. You know the work. Now you're just serving more people at once.

The platform gives you everything you need: payments, courses, events, gamification. The only thing it doesn't give you is the decision to start. That's on you. 🔥
